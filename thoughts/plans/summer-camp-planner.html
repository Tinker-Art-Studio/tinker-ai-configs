Goal

When Christie plans Summer 2027, she can open one screen and see, for every Summer 2026 camp, whether it earned a spot again and about how many seats to offer. She can also see which themes (animals, fantasy, sewing & fashion…) families wanted most.

The method, in plain terms

TermMeaning
DemandSeats filled + ½ × people waitlisted. Half, because one child can sit on several waitlists or get into a different week. (Christie's call, 2026-09-26.) The weight lives in one constant, WAIT_WEIGHT = 0.5.
Suggested 2027 seatsDemand, rounded up to a whole seat. This is a raw seat count, not a number of weeks, because rooms differ (SoBo Studio holds 18, Mountain View Workshop holds 8). Christie turns seats into weeks and rooms.
Verdict Grow — demand ≥ 110% of seats offered
Keep — 90–110%
Shrink — 60–90% (suggest ≈ the seats actually filled)
Rethink — under 60% full with nobody waiting
Sold out, no waitlistLabelled Keep — ceiling unknown. When a camp sold out and nobody waitlisted, we can't tell whether it could have filled more seats.
Thin dataA camp with only one section or fewer than 12 seats is flagged "one data point". It still gets a verdict, but it's marked so Christie doesn't read too much into it.

Decisions to confirm in review

D1 — Merge the same camp across locations and ages

These names count as one camp: "Tinker @ Coal Creek: Adopt-a-Pet Camp" and "Adopt-a-Pet Camp", and "Dino Camp! (Ages 4.5 - 7)" and "Dino Camp". Merging strips the location prefix, age range, and trailing "!". Each merged row still has a per-location breakdown underneath, because location matters. Cat Lovers, Adopt-a-Pet, Galaxy and Detectives all did worse at Coal Creek, and that shouldn't be blamed on the theme.
Names that are genuinely different offerings stay separate: "Wheel Throwing: Beginners" vs "Intermediate", and "Teen Digital Art Camp" vs "Digital Art Camp". This uses a new normalizer, campKey(). The existing norm() merges Beginners with Intermediate and is used for prior-term matching, so it stays untouched.

D2 — Summer themes come from a named list, not keyword guessing

Keyword topics put 102 of 166 summer sections into "Mixed & themed", because summer names are themes, not media. Summer gets its own explicit camp → theme list (SUMMER_THEMES). The first draft is below and Christie edits it. Any camp not on the list shows under "Not sorted yet" in plain sight, so a new 2027 camp name is never silently misfiled.

D3 — What's in scope

In: every Summer 2026 camp and 2-day camp (kind c, plus "2-Day Camp:" one-offs).
Out: Lunchtime Artists Club (already excluded from camp analytics everywhere), drop-ins, Friday Art Nights, and the 11 summer semester-style classes (Intro to Oils etc.). Those have their own tables. A toggle "Include summer classes & workshops" can add them back.

D4 — Where it lives

It's a new section at the bottom of the Analytics tab, called "Plan next summer". It always reads the most recent finished summer (Summer 2026 today), whatever terms the chips above select, because it answers only one question. Once Summer 2027 is finished, the same section reads 2027 automatically. It also shows the change against 2026 for camps that ran both years. That is the payoff of a second year of data.

D5 — Small fix bundled in: "Oils" isn't in Painting

The Painting keyword rule matches oil\b, so "Intro to Oils" and "Oils II" fall into "Mixed & themed" in the existing Topic grouping. Change it to oils?\b. This is one character, and it affects every term's Topic view (correctly).
